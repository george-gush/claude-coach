### How it actually behaves — the athlete's own words, 2026-09-19

> "It would hurt if I started activity without a warm-up. [...] Generally I don't
> feel that much pain. I think the pain would come if I start doing exercises
> without warming up the knee properly. [...] At some point it just feels like a
> bit of swelling and a bit of discomfort."

> "I can't push too hard in runs because I feel like the strength isn't there,
> and the load will all go on the knee."

**Two things follow from this, and they drive the whole programme:**

1. **It is a warm-up problem, not a damage problem.** Cold start → pain. Warmed
   up → no real pain. So the warm-up block is mandatory before every run, ride
   and gym session. See `strength.md` §3.
2. **His self-diagnosis is correct.** Weak supporting muscle means the joint
   takes the load. Building quadriceps and hip strength is the treatment, not a
   precaution. Randomised trials show exercise therapy matches surgery for
   degenerative meniscal tears (Tier A — see `strength.md` §2).

### Stop signals — swelling outranks pain

Full traffic-light rules in `strength.md` §5. Short version:

- **GREEN** — no swelling, ≤2/10, settles in 2 h → progress
- **AMBER** — 3/10, morning stiffness clearing by midday, or slight fullness → hold load, cut lower volume 30%
- **RED** — visible swelling, ≥4/10, pain past midday, any catching or locking → isometrics only, tell the physio

Joint swelling switches the quadriceps off (arthrogenic muscle inhibition).
That is why swelling, not pain, is the primary signal.
