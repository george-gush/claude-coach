## Findings
1. **Heart-rate drift 16.5 %.** Runs 1–6: 2.13 m/s at HR 128. Runs 8–13: 2.08 m/s at
   HR 150 — slightly slower for 22 bpm more. Above 5 % means the effort was not
   sustainable aerobically. Two causes: the heat (dew point 24 °C), and the surge.
2. **The surge in run 7 (6:54/km, HR 157, max 167)** came from a longer stride
   (88 cm vs 77–80), not faster steps. HR never came back under 145 afterwards, and
   Garmin's performance condition fell from 0 to −6 over the rest of the run.
3. **Cadence is good and steady: 160–166 spm** at 7:30–8:00/km. It **collapsed only in
   run 12 (147 spm, power 186 W, GCT 300 ms)** — the 8.1–8.6 km "cooked" point.
4. **Walk breaks work:** HR falls ~20 bpm in 60 s early on; only 10–12 bpm late. The two
   walks he ran (5 and 13) gave no recovery at all.
5. Ground contact 285–300 ms and vertical ratio ~10 % are normal for this pace;
   contact time lengthens with fatigue (285 → 300).
6. vs 11 Oct (7.2 km, 32–35 °C, humidity 47 %): HR 135 avg then, 140 now over 40 %
   more distance in more humid air — fitness is moving the right way.

## Race-day rules from this run
- First half of the run: **HR ≤ 150 on run segments**, whatever the pace says. No surges.
- **Walk every walk break.**
- When tired: **shorten the stride, keep cadence ≥ 160.**
- Heat plan: water on the head and neck at every aid station; drink in every walk.
- Fresh run-walk in heat ≈ 83 min; off a 40 km bike expect ≈ 88–92 min.
