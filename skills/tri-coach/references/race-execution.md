# Race Execution — Taper, Pacing and Race Day

Race: Olympic distance, **Saturday 14 November 2026**. Abu Dhabi region.
Event name, start time, venue and wetsuit rules still **(ask)**.

Write the race plan to `athlete/plans/2026-11-14_race-plan.md`. Draft it about
three weeks out (mid-October), review it together, and finalise it in race week.

---

## 1. Taper (Tier A — one of the best-evidenced things in sport)

Expect roughly 1–3% performance gain from a proper taper. The shape that works:

- **Cut volume 40–60%** from the last normal week. Decay it progressively, not
  in one step.
- **Keep the intensity.** This is the classic mistake. Quality sessions stay at
  race-relevant intensity — they just get shorter, with fewer repeats.
- **Keep the frequency.** Drop at most one session. Frequency preserves feel,
  especially in the swim.
- **Length: 7–10 days** for short course. Olympic distance does not need two full
  weeks, and this athlete's fitness is still building — a long taper costs more
  than it gains. Start 2 November.
- **Form target on race day: about +5 to +15.** His form has been permanently
  negative, so simply getting it positive will feel dramatic. Warn him.
- **Warn him about the taper feeling.** Feeling flat, heavy and anxious in the
  first taper week is normal. It is not a sign of lost fitness and it is not a
  reason to add training back.
- **Strength:** last heavy gym session about 10 days out (≈ 4 November). After
  that, movement and isometrics only. Do not drop the gym entirely — his knee
  depends on that strength being present on race day.

## 2. Pacing

**Even or slightly conservative pacing wins.** Almost every blow-up is caused by
going out too hard. Set explicit **early caps**, not just targets.

### Swim — 1.5 km
- Controlled first 200 m, then settle. The first two minutes will feel harder
  than they should. That is normal and it passes.
- Target about 2:00 /100 m → roughly 30 minutes. Add time for open water,
  sighting and a group start.
- Draft legally on feet or hip if he can. It is free speed.
- Seed himself honestly at the start. Going off in a fast pack costs more than
  it gains.

### Bike — 40 km
- Roughly **0.88–0.92 of FTP**, so about **220–230 W** off FTP 250 W.
- **Cap the first 10 minutes** at or below 210 W. Legs are always willing out of
  transition and this is where the run gets destroyed.
- Keep the effort even. Do not surge on climbs; do not soft-pedal downhill.
- Expect about 75–85 minutes.

### Run — 10 km
- **This is the discipline that needs the most conservative plan**, because it is
  the least developed.
- Target roughly **9:30–10:30 /km**. Expect 95–105 minutes.
- **State the first-kilometre number explicitly in the race plan.** The first
  1–2 km off the bike must be deliberately slower than goal pace. Every plan
  states this figure.
- **Planned walk breaks are a strategy, not a failure.** Walking the aid stations
  is a legitimate and often faster approach at this fitness level. Plan them in
  advance so they are a decision rather than a collapse.

### Do not predict a finish time
He asked on 2026-09-19 not to be given projected completion times, and he is
checking the cut-offs himself. Give him **pacing targets and effort caps per
discipline**. Do not total them up into a predicted finish unless he asks.

## 3. Heat

Gulf in mid-November is normally warm rather than brutal, but check the forecast
from two weeks out.

- **Adjust pace expectations, not effort.** Hold target heart rate and accept a
  slower pace.
- If the forecast is above about 25 °C, add heat preparation: 10–14 days of
  60–90 min sessions with raised core temperature, finishing near race day
  (Tier A for hot races). He is training in Abu Dhabi already, so he is largely
  acclimated — verify rather than assume.
- Pre-cooling with cold fluid or ice before the start has Tier A support in hot
  conditions.
- Water temperature decides the wetsuit question. Warm Gulf water in November
  often means a non-wetsuit swim. Confirm the rule and, if it is non-wetsuit,
  make sure he has swum 1.5 km without one before race day.

## 4. Race week

- **Extend sleep all week.** The night two nights before matters more than the
  night before. Given his sleep history, this is the highest-value thing in the
  week.
- **Practise nothing new.** No new food, no new kit, no new pacing idea.
- **Openers, the day before:** 20–30 min easy with 3–4 × 60–90 s at race effort.
  Primes the system at no cost.
- **Final checks:** race plan read together, kit laid out, transition rehearsed,
  warm-up written down with actual clock times.
- **Knee:** Block 0 warm-up on race morning is not optional. Build it into the
  written warm-up with a time attached to it.

## 6. After the race

- **Debrief within a few days.** Result against the written plan, discipline by
  discipline. Fill in the post-race section. Copy the lessons to
  `athlete/reviews/` and `athlete/decisions.md`.
- **Recovery.** Roughly one easy or rest day per hour of racing. No intensity
  until sleep, appetite and motivation are all back to normal.
- **Then start the deferred work:** the plyometric progression that was held back
  because of the knee, beginning with low-amplitude ankle work. See
  `strength.md` §8.
- Record how the taper went — length, form achieved, how he felt, the result —
  so the next one is built on his own history rather than a template.
